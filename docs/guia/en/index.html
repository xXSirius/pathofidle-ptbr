01How the game works

If you're coming from a regular action RPG, here's the first thing to unlearn: you don't control combat here.

Path of Idle is an idle ARPG. You don't click a skill, dodge, or aim. Your characters fight on their own, following automatic logic. What you do is build the machine: choose characters, skills, gear, and upgrades. Then you just watch — or close the game and come back later, since it accumulates offline rewards.

That changes how much decisions weigh. In a regular ARPG, a skilled player can make up for a bad build. Here there's no such thing: the build is the whole game. That's why this guide spends so much time on rules that look like details — they're what separates a character that stalls at chapter 8 from one that clears chapter 20.

The core loop

You progress through the Campaign, split into 20 chapters. Each chapter has several sites; clearing all of them unlocks the Boss. Meanwhile, the resources you collect get invested into the city, which is where permanent power lives.

Golden rule right away: every chapter's Boss always drops a Set piece, and treasure sites always drop a Legendary — but treasure sites can only be challenged once. Fight the Boss as soon as it unlocks; it's your reliable source of the pieces that actually matter.

The four activities

Campaign

The main progression. Unlocks chapters, raises your level cap, and generates most of your resources and gear.

Divine Tower

Permanently boosts Campaign resource rewards. Each floor gives Soul Shards. The Audience Chamber grants a Divinity effect that only applies on that floor.

Exploration

The tap you can aim: pick an island type and go after the specific resource you're missing.

Doom

End-game content, with its own modifiers and dedicated stages.

Prioritize the Tower early. Its bonus is permanent and compounding — every hour you don't raise it is an hour everything else earns less. You can go straight for the floor's Boss, but the fight is much harder without the Divinity effect.

02Worshiper, Godsworn & teams

This is where the #1 confusion for beginners lives: the game has two different things that look the same.

Worshiper
That's you. The level in the top-right corner of the screen — your account, the cult you lead. Its cap is 5 × the chapter number (at chapter 7, cap 35).
Godsworn
Those are the characters. Each one you pull from the Prison is a Godsworn, with its own class, rarity, level, skills, and gear. They're who actually fights.

Raising your Worshiper level does two things: it increases how many Godsworn you can have at once, and it improves offline rewards. The cap starts at 5 Godsworn and reaches 25 at level 100 (+1 every 5 levels).

The three teams

You don't play with a single character — you play with teams of 3 Godsworn, and you can have three teams running different adventures at the same time:

TeamUnlocks atGodsworn
Team IFrom the start3
Team IIChapter 43
Team IIIChapter 103

In other words: by the end you have 9 Godsworn deployed, on three independent fronts — one team on the Campaign, another on the Tower, another on Exploration.

How to build a team of 3

There's positioning inside a team, and it works on two layers.

The order of the 3 slots sets the starting formation. Whoever you put in the first slot spawns in front, the second in the middle, the third in back — three fixed positions on the field. You control this by dragging Godsworn between the team's slots.

But "the frontmost ally" is recalculated constantly. During the fight, the game doesn't look at the slot number: it ranks the three by each one's actual position on the field at that instant. Since units move on their own — and every skill has its own cast range — whoever uses a short-range skill advances and takes the front, while long-range users fall back. The starting formation is the starting point; skill range is what holds it (or flips it).

And that matters, because a lot of skills and affixes explicitly target "the frontmost ally":

Transferred damage
Several affixes transfer part of the damage taken to the frontmost ally (from 12% to 43%, depending on the affix).
Shields
Skills like Ice Barrier apply their shield to the frontmost ally specifically.
Area buffs
Effects like Honor Guard hit "the three frontmost allies."
Practical composition rule: put the tankiest one up front — usually the Warrior (Plate armor and the game's best Health growth) or the Cleric (also Plate). Squishier damage dealers (Mage, Priest) go in back. The classic team that works: 1 that tanks hits + 1 that deals damage + 1 support/auras.

And yes, specialized teams are absolutely worth it. Since Auras and "all allies" buffs only count within the team, grouping characters that reinforce each other pays off more than spreading out. Specializations that make sense:

Advance team
Your strongest, with the best gear, pushing the Campaign to open chapters — which is what raises everyone's level cap.
Resource team
Doesn't need to be strong, just needs to survive. Sits on Exploration farming whatever resource is bottlenecking you.
Tower team
The Tower has enemies with their own patterns. A team built with the right resistances can clear floors the advance team can't.
Watch out for the common mistake: don't spread your best items across all three teams "to keep everyone average." Concentrate on the advance team — it's the one that opens chapters, and opening a chapter raises the level cap for all nine Godsworn. Average teams stall everyone together.

03Daily routine

That "there's too much stuff" feeling disappears once you realize almost everything can be automated, and only five decisions actually need your attention.

Step 1 — Set it up once and forget it

Recommended filter
In the auto-sell settings, turn on the filter recommendations. The game starts adjusting what it sells by itself, based on your chapter. It's the single thing that fixes inventory clutter for good.
Auto Battle
Unlocks after chapter one. Top-right corner of the adventure slot.
Stop on death
Turn it on in both places (challenge and Exploration). The game itself marks it as recommended — without it you get stuck in a loss loop without noticing.
Return to chapter
"Return to max chapter on finish" for the Tower and Exploration. Keeps the team from sitting idle.
Auto-advance
Auto-advance stage, auto-boss, and auto-next-chapter.
If you only do one thing from this section: turn on the recommended sell filter.

Step 2 — The cycle of every session

  1. Collect offline rewardsThere's a cap on hours — go past it and it stops accumulating. First thing to do when you open the game.
  2. Empty your bagOpen the equipment chests (hold right-click to open fast). With the filter on, junk is already sold automatically.
  3. MarketCheck the Orders: they ask for a piece of specific quality and slot, pay in rare resources, and auto-search your bag and Warehouse on their own.
  4. PrisonRefresh looking for high rarity — but read section 07 first, there's an important rule here.
  5. TempleSpend Blood on Blessings. Each one has a 4% chance of granting a Talent point.
  6. WorkshopForge and Refine only what's final. Resources spent on a transitional piece are resources lost.
  7. SteleCheck the stage tasks — completing them early unlocks bigger upgrades on the other buildings.
  8. Let it runSplit your teams: advance on the Campaign, the others on the Tower and Exploration.

Step 3 — What to keep and what to sell

This is the exact rule the game's own filter uses. With T as your chapter's level cap (5 × chapter):

RaritySell belowException — always keep
Commonlevel T − 7With 3 sockets
Finelevel T − 12With 2 sockets or 2 Fine affixes
Rarelevel T − 17With 2 Fine affixes or 3+ Rare affixes
LegendaryNever auto-sell
SetNever auto-sell
UniqueNever auto-sell
MythicNever auto-sell
The most important exception: Common gear with 3 sockets is the only raw material for Runewords — and a Runeword is what gives your Godsworn a skill from another class. The official filter only flags "0, 1, and 2 sockets" for sale on purpose, sparing the 3-socket ones. If you configure the filter by hand and check "3", you'll sell off the most valuable thing a white item can be without even noticing.

Step 4 — Where resources come from and go

Gold

Campaign and Exploration → Bewitch prisoners, buy at the Market, Forge at the Workshop.

Blood of the Old Gods

Campaign and Exploration → Bless Godsworn at the Temple, Torment prisoners.

Bone

Soul Shards

Divine Tower floors → Infuse classes.

Awareness: only goes up with Rare or better items (Rare gives 10, Legendary/Set/Unique 50, Mythic 200). Common and Fine give zero — let the filter sell those freely. What Awareness is and what it's for: section 12.

04Casting priority

The single most important mechanic in the game, and the one fewest people understand. How the AI decides which skill to use.

Since you don't control combat, there's a fixed order: a Godsworn always tries the highest-priority skill that's ready first. The order is always this:

  1. SupportHealing, shields, summoning allies and minions. Highest priority — always fires first.
  2. UtilityBuffs on allies, debuffs on enemies.
  3. MasteryThe heavy-hitting skills. This is where your damage lives.
  4. BasicThe "normal attack." Lowest priority — only fires when nothing above is available.
The consequence that breaks builds: every high-priority skill you add steals time from your Mastery. If you fill the bar with Support and Utility, the skill that's supposed to kill things almost never fires. This is cause #1 of "my build doesn't deal damage."

Build it like this: one Mastery as your main damage source, one Basic chosen for the effect it generates (Mettle, Haste, or Sunder — not for its damage), and only the Utility/Support skills whose effect really multiplies the build. If you can't explain in one sentence why a skill is there, it's probably in the way.

Only one Basic. The three skills at the bottom of the Talent screen are fixed as Basics, and you can only have one active.

Martial × Spell

There's a second axis, parallel to priority: every skill is either Martial (combat technique) or Spell (casting). That matters because attributes are split by axis — Martial Speed and Spell Speed are different stats.

The game's most common waste: using a "Spell Damage" item on a Martial build. It does absolutely nothing.

05Boosts & Afflictions

Two stacking systems that almost every good build leans on. Both: max 5 stacks, 4 seconds each, with duration tracked separately per stack.

A "stack" is one layer of an effect. If a skill grants 1 stack of Mettle and you use it five times in a row, you have 5 — the cap. Since each one lasts 4 seconds on its own, keeping the cap requires applying it steadily, not all at once.

Boosts bonuses on you

Mettle
+5% All Damage and +5% Physical Defense per stack. At cap: +25% damage.
Haste
+5% Martial Speed and +5% Dodge Value per stack.
Fortitude
+3% Primary Attributes (Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence) and +6% Crit Resistance per stack.

Afflictions penalties on the enemy

Sunder
−10% target's All Defense and +5% Crit Damage taken, per stack. At cap: −50% defense.
Slow
−6% Movement Speed and −5% Skill Speed on the target.
Wound
−10% Healing received and −10% Health Regen on the target.
Shatter
Reduces the target's Shield effectiveness and speeds up its decay.
Sunder is close to mandatory. Five stacks strip half the target's defense — no item in the game gives a multiplier this size this cheaply. Any damage build improves by fitting in a Sunder source. Warrior has Cruel Hook, Mage has Shockwave, and Priest has it built into their own Basic (Frost Assault).

Dispel & Purge

Dispel
Removes all Buffs from the target — except Boosts.
Purge
Removes all Debuffs — except Afflictions.

In other words: Boosts and Afflictions can't be cleansed. That's why stacking builds are so reliable against bosses, which usually have effect removal.

06States & Reservation

Four states condition dozens of affixes — and there's a Reservation trick that turns one of them permanent.

First things first: Reservation is when something (typically an Aura) locks up a fixed slice of your Health or Mana while it's active. That slice becomes unavailable — as if your bar had shrunk.

StateDefault conditionWhat Reservation does
HealthyHealth above 70%Reserving more than 30% Health, you never count as Healthy.
BloodiedHealth below 30%Reserving more than 70%, you count as Bloodied at all times.
FullMana above 70%Reserving more than 30% Mana, you never count as Full.
DepletedMana below 30%Reserving more than 70%, you count as Depleted permanently.
The Bloodied build trick. There are lots of affixes like "while Bloodied, +X" and "Crit vs Bloodied targets." Normally taking advantage of them means walking around at low health — risky. But by reserving more than 70% of your Health you become permanently Bloodied with a full bar, turning on all those affixes with no risk. That's exactly how the Bloodied Blade and Bloodied Slam builds work.

Auras, Allies & Minions

Aura
Activates on its own in combat and gives a continuous bonus to the team. Consumes Health or Mana as a reservation and doesn't stack with another identical one.
Ally
A strong unit that already starts on the field, with no duration limit — but it doesn't revive if it dies.
Minion
Summoned by a skill. Individually weak, with a duration and a summon cap.

Allies and Minions inherit part of your attributes. That's why an Ally build scales with your Physical Attack, not theirs — gearing yourself is gearing the creature.

07Getting good Godsworn

A Godsworn's rarity is the decision that compounds the most across the whole game — and it doesn't depend on luck the way it looks like it does.

Every Godsworn comes from the Prison: you refresh the prisoner list and use Bewitch (costs Gold) to convert one into a Godsworn. What almost nobody notices is how much rarity actually changes:

RarityTalent PointsGrowth per levelStarting attributeLegendary traitsCost
Common1200100001×
Fine2250125002×
Rare3300150015×
Legendary43501750212×
Mythic54002000330×
Read the growth column again. A Mythic gains double the attribute per level of a Common, and has 5× more Talent Points. With a level cap of 100, that's not "a little better" — it's a character worth two. It costs 30× more Gold, and it's still worth it: Gold comes back, 99 levels of lost growth don't.

Why you only ever see Common and Fine

It's not bad luck. By default the Prison only produces Common and Fine — it's hard-coded that way. Rare-or-better prisoners don't appear until you buy the matching upgrade in the Prison's Renovation tree.
RenovationCostRequirementChance once unlocked
Rare Prisoner400—~2.3%
Legendary Prisoner1,000Rare unlocked~0.26%
Mythic Prisoner2,200Legendary unlocked~0.02%

That answers "when do Godsworn above Rare start showing up": when you buy the Renovation. And looking at the odds, it's clear that waiting for a Mythic to drop isn't a plan — 0.02% is one in five thousand refreshes. There are Renovations that boost the higher-rarity odds by +30% to +50%, but even then Mythic stays out of reach on pure luck.

The real path: Warp

This is where the Prison gets interesting. Besides Bewitch, there are two other actions:

Torment
Spends Blood to re-roll the Godsworn's primary attribute growth — in other words, which attribute they'll be good at.
Warp
Spends resources to try to raise rarity by one step. This is the real path to Legendary and Mythic.
WarpSuccess chanceRelative cost
Common → Fine100%1×
Fine → Rare40%2×
Rare → Legendary20%4×
Legendary → Mythic10%8×

In other words: you manufacture the good Godsworn instead of waiting for one. Grab a cheap one and Warp it up. Warp also respects the cap unlocked by the Renovation — without the Mythic Renovation purchased, no Warp goes past Legendary.

The risk: Torment and Warp move the prisoner's Conviction (a 0–100 bar), and the extremes are final:
• Conviction 0 = Breakdown — the prisoner is lost, can no longer be operated on or Bewitched.
• Conviction 100 = Frenzy — locks the actions, but Bewitch cost is cut in half.

There are Renovations that give a 10% and 15% chance for Conviction to not change at all — those are what make this safe to do. Buy them before investing heavily in a prisoner.
The Frenzy trick: since Frenzy halves the Bewitch cost while locking the actions, the right order is: first Warp and Torment until you're satisfied, then (if Conviction is close to 100) push it into Frenzy and recruit at half price.

Decide your build before recruiting

Preferred Skills. You can mark Talent skills as preferred, and prisoners get a higher chance of already being born with them. How many you can mark depends on your Stele level.

That flips the usual order: first pick the build, mark its skills, and only then start refreshing the Prison. Without that, you're recruiting blind and hoping.

10The 8 equipment slots

Answers the right question: no, the rarest item isn't automatically the best — and no, you don't wear all of one type.

Every Godsworn has 8 slots:

SlotQty.Note
Weapon2Two simultaneous weapons, one of each type for your class
Amulet1
Ring1
Helmet1
Torso1
Gloves1
Boots1
Detail a lot of people miss: it's two weapons at once, not a choice between two. That's why the Mercenary has the Double Strike mastery, which deals extra damage when both weapons are the same type.

The slot budget

Here's the answer to "does every item have a purpose?" Yes — because Sets require 2 or 4 pieces to activate their bonuses, and you have 8 slots to spread around. That creates a budget:

4 + 4 free
One full set (2- and 4-piece bonuses active) and 4 free slots for standalone Legendary, Unique, or Mythic pieces. The most common layout.
4 + 2 + 2
One full set plus the 2-piece bonus from another set, and 2 free.
2 + 2 + 4 free
Two partial sets, taking just the 2-piece bonus from each. Useful when the 2-piece bonuses are strong and the 4-piece ones don't fit the build.
Why "all Mythic" is a bad build. Eight standalone Mythics activate zero Set bonuses — and Set bonuses are what make a build exist (they're what turns "I hit things" into "I hit things and that triggers something else"). A full 4-piece Set (Green) beats four loose Mythics in most builds.

The mental rule: the set is the engine, the free slots are the fuel. First lock in the 4 pieces that turn on the build; then fill the rest with the best you have in the attributes the build wants.

How to compare two items

  1. Is it part of my set?If so, it almost always wins — the bonus is worth more than raw numbers.
  2. Are the affixes on the build's list?Every official build lists its "preferred attributes." An item with two affixes from that list beats one with a higher base number and off-list affixes.
  3. Is it the right axis?Martial or Spell. Being on the wrong axis zeroes out the affix's value.
  4. Only then the base numberIt's a tiebreaker, not the first filter.

The four systems

Forging

Improves base attributes. The cap rises with the Workshop. A Forged piece also sells for more.

Socketing

Adds affixes. Only Common (up to 3) and Fine (up to 2) roll sockets, and only above level 20.

Refining

Common → Legendary; Magic → Set; Legendary → Mythic (and here, failure destroys the item).

Corruption

Risky reroll, with a limited number of uses per item.

Classic mistake: a socketed item can't be Refined. If you plan on Refining, do it before socketing a rune — otherwise you lock the piece at its current rarity forever.

12Awareness & Affix Seals

The only system in the game that lets you aim at what drops. Everything else is luck with a filter — the seal changes the roll itself.

Every piece of equipment has an entry in the Codex, and each entry has its own Awareness bar. Here's the part that trips people up: Awareness isn't a global account number — it's per item. The "Iron Axe" has its own Awareness, "Azra's Crown" has its own.

How Awareness goes up

By obtaining that same piece again. The better the rarity of the copy that drops, the more Awareness you get:

Rarity obtainedAwareness gained
Common · Fine · Magic0
Rare10
Legendary · Set · Unique50
Mythic200

Once the bar fills, that item's Awareness level rises and the bar resets. The level cap is set by your Stele level — that's why the Stele shows up throughout this guide.

What it's for: sealing affixes

With enough Awareness on a piece, you can seal affixes on it through the Codex. A sealed affix stops appearing on that item — permanently.

In practice: you remove the affixes that don't fit your build from the roll pool. The next copies of that item drop with a cleaner pool — better odds of getting what you actually want, without relying on pure luck.

The cost is Awareness level + Blood, and it scales fast:

50 levels total. And the game's own warning holds: the more affixes you've already sealed on that piece, the more Awareness the next seal requires.

The consequence that changes how you play: farming the same Set piece repeatedly is not a waste, even after you already have one. Every copy raises that item's Awareness and gets you closer to being able to seal off its junk affixes. It's the opposite of the "I already have this one, sell it" instinct.

14Use a ready-made build, or make your own?

Short answer: use the ready-made ones now, and make your own once you understand why they work.

The game's 39 builds aren't generic suggestions — each one is built around a specific Set bonus, and most literally say "centered on Set gear." They exist because the game has triggers: a 4-piece bonus that makes skill A fire skill B. Figuring these combos out yourself, by cross-referencing 18 sets against 72 masteries, is a lot of work.

Use ready-made builds when

You're learning
They teach you the game's grammar: which skill triggers which, which attributes travel together.
You're short on testing time
Testing a build here is slow — you need to collect the set pieces first. A ready-made build saves you from finding out at chapter 12 that the idea doesn't hold together.
You want a clear target
The build becomes your filter: which set to hunt, which affixes to keep, which skills to mark as preferred.

Make your own when

You've already run one
After taking an official build to completion, you understand the pattern and can predict what fits together.
You found an unused trigger
There are 72 masteries and not all of them show up in the official builds. Combos outside the list exist.
You're thinking about the team
Official builds describe one Godsworn. Since you play in teams of 3, the synergy between the three is your territory — the game doesn't suggest anything there.
The recommended path: pick an official Easy-difficulty build for your main Godsworn and take it to completion. Meanwhile, use the other two team slots to experiment. You learn at low risk, and the team keeps advancing.
About having fun: the difficulty rating measures how much specific gear the build needs, not how fun it is. A "Hard" Priest Totem build is perfectly playable — it just needs more pieces aligned to shine. If a concept appeals to you, chase it: the game is long enough for you to run a solid build and an experimental one at the same time, on different teams.

17The city

Long-term power doesn't come from loot — it comes from here.

Temple

Blessing and Baptism. Each Blessing has a 4% chance to grant 1 Talent point. Blessing level tops out at 50 with the building maxed.

Workshop

Forging and socketing. Its level sets the Forging cap and lowers prices.

Stele

Stage quests, the Awareness cap, and the Preferred Skills cap. Completing stages early unlocks bigger upgrades on the other buildings.

Prison

Where Godsworn are born. Its Renovations unlock the high rarities — see section 07.

Market

Buy, sell, and Orders (turn in a specific piece, get rare resources). Also has the Vault, which lists high-quality gear for Runes.

Sanctuary

Special combinations, like Faith Skulls into Crystallized Skulls.

Warehouse

Extended storage and the Vault: a separate high-quality store with stacking. Keeps your bag from clogging up.

Golem

Attribute bonuses that apply to every Godsworn of that class — account-wide progression. The cap rises with your Worshiper level.

Inspire grants a Godsworn a Mastery from another class. The amount and cap rise with upgrades — it's the main source of hybrid builds, alongside Runewords.

18How to get good

A summary of what actually moves the needle, in order of impact.

  1. Respect casting prioritySupport > Utility > Mastery > Basic. If your Mastery isn't firing, you have too many high-priority skills. Cause #1 of a build with no damage.
  2. Buy the Prison's rarity RenovationsWithout them, Rare-or-better prisoners don't exist. After that, use Warp to manufacture a good Godsworn instead of waiting for one.
  3. Fit in a Sunder sourceFive stacks = −50% target defense. The cheapest multiplier in the game.
  4. Concentrate on your advance teamOpening a chapter raises the level cap for all nine Godsworn. Spreading gear across three average teams stalls everyone.
  5. Chase the set, not the rarityFour Green pieces that turn on a trigger beat four loose Mythics.
  6. Raise the Divine Tower earlyPermanent, compounding bonus.
  7. Refine before socketingA socketed item can no longer be Refined.
  8. Don't play ahead of your levelLevel Suppression punishes both ways.
  9. Pick your Basic for its effectIt exists to generate Mettle, Haste, or Sunder — not to deal damage.
  10. Mark Preferred Skills before recruitingDeciding the build first is what makes the Prison work for you.
